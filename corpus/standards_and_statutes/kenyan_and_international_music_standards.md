# Kenyan & International Music Contract Standards, Guidelines & Statutory Compendium

## Part I: Statutory Provisions (Republic of Kenya)

### 1. Kenya Copyright Act (Cap. 130, Laws of Kenya)
- **Section 22 & Section 26 (Economic Rights in Musical Works):** The author of an original musical work holds the exclusive right to control the reproduction in any material form, translation, adaptation, distribution to the public, broadcasting, and communication to the public of the whole work or a substantial part thereof.
- **Section 28 (Rights in Sound Recordings):** The producer of a sound recording (the person or entity that first fixes the sounds, typically funded by the artist in independent sessions or the record label under a recording contract) controls the direct or indirect reproduction, distribution, commercial rental, and making available of the sound recording via digital networks.
- **Section 30 & Section 30A (Performer's Rights & Single Equitable Remuneration):**
  - Under **Section 30**, no person may, without the consent of the performer, broadcast or communicate to the public an unfixed performance, make a fixation of a live performance, or reproduce a fixation made without consent.
  - Under **Section 30A**, if a sound recording published for commercial purposes is used directly for broadcasting or other communication to the public (including radio, television, public venues, and telecom ringback tones such as Skiza Tunes), a single equitable remuneration shall be paid by the user and shared equally (50% / 50%) between the performer(s) and the producer of the sound recording through an accredited Collective Management Organization (CMO).
- **Section 32 (Moral Rights of Paternity and Integrity):**
  - Independently of the author's economic rights and even after the transfer of those rights, the author (and performer under Section 30) retains the right to claim authorship of the work (**Right of Paternity**) and to object to any distortion, mutilation, or other modification of, or other derogatory action in relation to, the said work which would be prejudicial to the author's honour or reputation (**Right of Integrity**).
  - Under Kenyan statute, moral rights are not assignable or transmissible during the lifetime of the author. Blanket contractual "waivers of all moral rights" or clauses allowing a counterparty to alter, parody, or train AI voice models on an artist's work without consent conflict with Section 32.
- **Section 33 (Assignments and Exclusive Licenses):**
  - An assignment of copyright or an exclusive license to do an act controlled by copyright has no legal effect unless it is in writing and signed by or on behalf of the assignor or licensor.
  - Where an agreement does not specify the duration or territory of an assignment, Kenyan copyright policy and KECOBO guidelines favour narrowly construed, term-limited licenses over perpetual worldwide assignments.

### 2. Performers Protection Act (Cap. 229A, Laws of Kenya)
- Prohibits making a record, film, broadcast, or audiovisual stream of a performer's live musical performance without the performer's prior written consent.
- Concert and festival booking contracts that bury a perpetual, uncompensated "live capture and commercial synchronization" clause inside general festival regulations strip the artist of statutory protections under Cap. 229A and Cap. 130 Section 30.

### 3. Income Tax Act (Cap. 470, Laws of Kenya) — Withholding Tax on Artists
- **Resident Withholding Tax (5%):** Payments to resident Kenyan musicians for contractual performance fees, brand endorsements, management/agency services above statutory thresholds, and domestic royalties are subject to a **five percent (5%)** Withholding Tax rate (20% for non-residents).
- **Mandatory KRA iTax Certificate:** The paying brand, promoter, or label must remit the deducted tax to the Kenya Revenue Authority (KRA) and furnish an official KRA iTax Withholding Tax Certificate to the artist within fourteen (14) days so the artist can claim the tax credit on their annual return.

### 4. Data Protection Act (No. 24 of 2019, Laws of Kenya)
- Voiceprints, facial geometry, and biometric likeness data used to train artificial intelligence ("Digital Twin") vocal or visual synthesis models constitute personal and sensitive biometric data requiring specific, informed, revocable written consent and separate compensation.

## Part II: KECOBO & WIPO Contract Benchmarks by Deal Type

### A. Exclusive Recording & 360-Degree Agreements (`recording`)
1. **Royalty Rate & Basis:** At least **15% to 20% of Gross / PPD Receipts** (or **50% of Net Receipts** after verified direct out-of-pocket costs).
2. **No Artificial Deductions:** Zero "packaging/container" deductions (20%–25%) or "free goods" allowances (15%) on digital streams, downloads, or Skiza Tunes.
3. **Recoupment & No Cross-Collateralization:** Recording advances and studio costs are recoupable solely from the Artist's master recording royalties—never from the Artist's mechanical publishing royalties, CMO performance distributions, live gigs, or brand endorsements unless the label actively provides funded management/booking services under a transparent schedule.
4. **Term, Options & Release Commitment:** Initial Term of 12–18 months (1 Album/EP) with at most 1–2 mutual or milestone-conditioned options. Guaranteed commercial release within 6 months of master delivery, failing which the Artist may terminate and reclaim the masters.
5. **Reversion of Masters:** Rights in the masters license-terminate or revert to the Artist 5 to 7 years after commercial release or upon full recoupment of recording costs.

### B. Personal Artist Management Agreements (`management`)
1. **Commission Rate & Exclusions:** **15% to 20% of Net Entertainment Receipts** actually received by the Artist. Recording budgets, music video production funds, tour support deficits, and live sound/band pass-through costs must be excluded from commissionable gross income.
2. **Term & Performance Milestones:** Initial term of 2 to 3 years, with renewal contingent on achieving objective minimum gross income targets for the Artist.
3. **Key-Man Protection:** Personal services of the named lead manager are essential; if the named manager leaves or ceases active day-to-day involvement, the Artist may terminate without penalty.
4. **Stepped Sunset Clause:** Post-term commission applies only to contracts executed during the Term and steps down over a maximum of 2 years (e.g., 10% in Year 1, 5% in Year 2, 0% thereafter)—never in perpetuity.
5. **Trust Account & Audit:** Manager must not commingle Artist funds in a general operating account; Artist has an annual CPA(K) audit right with audit costs reimbursed if a discrepancy of 5% or more is found.

### C. Music Publishing & Co-Publishing Agreements (`publishing`)
1. **"At Source" Accounting & Writer's Share Preservation:** Royalties must be calculated **"At Source"** in the country of collection. The **Writer's Share (minimum 50% of public performance and broadcast royalties)** must always be paid directly to the songwriter by their CMO (MCSK) and never assigned to or recouped by the publisher.
2. **Administration vs. Perpetual Assignment:** Independent songwriters should grant a 3-to-5-year Administration license (10%–20% admin fee) or a term-limited 50/50 Co-Publishing license rather than a 100% perpetual copyright assignment.
3. **Exploitation Window & Reversion:** Any composition not commercially recorded, synchronized, or exploited within 24 months of delivery reverts 100% to the songwriter.
4. **Creative Sync Approvals:** Prior written consent of the songwriter is required before synchronizing a composition in political campaigns, religious promotions, alcohol, tobacco, gambling/betting advertisements, or AI training datasets.

### D. Brand Partnership, Influencer & Commercial Synchronization (`synchronization` / `general_music`)
1. **Gross Fee & Payment Schedule:** Guaranteed, non-recoupable Gross Fee paid 50% within 7–14 days of signing (before filming/recording) and 50% within 14–30 days of deliverable completion.
2. **Production & Agency Costs:** Brand bears 100% of studio, filming, styling, agency commission, and paid media-boosting costs; zero production costs are recoupable from the Artist's fee.
3. **Term-Limited License vs. Buyout:** Artist retains 100% copyright in the underlying composition and master recording, granting the Brand a non-exclusive or campaign-specific synchronization license strictly for the Campaign Term (e.g., 3–12 months) and Territory (e.g., Kenya / East Africa), with a 14–30 day post-term wind-down for paid ads.
4. **Narrow Category Exclusivity:** Exclusivity applies strictly to direct competitors in the specific product sub-category (e.g., mobile handsets, carbonated energy drinks) during the active paid Term only, with no uncompensated post-term "Cooling-Off Period" and full freedom to perform at multi-sponsor festivals.

### E. Studio Producer, Beat Lease & Split-Sheet Agreements (`producer`)
1. **Master Ownership & Session Fees:** Where the Artist pays the agreed studio production fee, the Artist owns 100% of the Master Sound Recording copyright (subject to agreed Producer royalty points of 3%–5% of gross receipts after recoupment).
2. **Mandatory Songwriting Split Sheet:** The parties execute a written Split Sheet upon completion of the session (typically 50% Producer for the instrumental music / 50% Artist for lyrics and vocal melody) for registration with KECOBO and MCSK.
3. **Exclusivity of Instrumental Beat:** Once an exclusive production fee is paid, the Producer covenants not to resell, lease, or license the underlying instrumental beat to any other artist.
4. **Sample Clearance Warranty:** Producer warrants that the beat is 100% original or that all third-party samples have been disclosed and cleared in writing, indemnifying the Artist against infringement claims.

### F. Digital Distribution & Skiza Aggregation Agreements (`distribution`)
1. **Copyright Retention:** Distribution agreements are strictly service/licensing arrangements; the Artist retains 100% of Master and Publishing copyrights.
2. **Transparent Revenue Split:** Artist receives 80%–90% of Gross Digital Receipts from DSPs (Spotify, Apple Music, Boomplay, Mdundo, YouTube Content ID) and 75%–85% of Net Telecom Receipts from CRBT platforms (Safaricom Skiza Tunes / Airtel Hello Tunes) after the telecom operator's statutory share, with zero hidden "subsidy" or packaging deductions.
3. **Monthly Statements & Fast Takedown:** Monthly accounting statements and M-Pesa/bank remittance within 30 days, and termination upon 30–60 days' written notice with complete DSP takedown within 14 days.

### G. Live Performance & Festival Booking Agreements (`live_performance`)
1. **50/50 Pre-Stage Settlement:** 50% non-refundable deposit upon signing and 50% balance paid prior to the Artist taking the stage (never contingent on promoter ticket-sales thresholds).
2. **Cancellation & Force Majeure Protections:** If the Promoter cancels within 14–30 days of the event without Artist default, 100% of the fee is payable; in a genuine Force Majeure event (severe weather, government ban, documented illness), the Artist retains the 50% deposit and the parties reschedule in good faith.
3. **Reasonable Radius Clause:** Any radius restriction is limited to public ticketed concerts within 30–50 km and 30–60 days of the festival, expressly excluding private corporate gigs, weddings, and club appearances.
4. **Live Recording & Merchandising:** Promoter may capture short clips (up to 60 seconds) for festival recap promotion only; any full-set VOD, pay-per-view livestream, or third-party brand commercial synchronization requires a separate written license and fee. Artist retains 80%–100% of on-site merchandise sales.
